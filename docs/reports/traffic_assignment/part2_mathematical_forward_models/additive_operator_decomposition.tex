\section{Additive Operator Decomposition}

The detailed and reduced models both become additive in demand once their
routing responses are fixed. This section isolates that common mathematical
structure. It explains why OD columns can be partitioned or compressed without
cutting journeys through the network, and why the forward and adjoint products
remain exact even when the full response matrix is never written explicitly.
In particular, sharding an implicit response controls construction and working
memory, but does not provide the same runtime benefit as constructing and
reusing compact measurement-response columns.

\subsection{Mathematical Sharding Identity}
\label{sec:mathematical-sharding}

Let the free OD-time cells be partitioned into disjoint index sets
$\mathcal C_1,\ldots,\mathcal C_S$.  Writing $P_s$ for the injection of the
coordinates in shard $s$ into the complete free vector gives
\[
  \bm f=\sum_{s=1}^{S}P_s\bm f_s,
  \qquad
  B\bm f=\sum_{s=1}^{S}B_s\bm f_s,
  \qquad B_s=BP_s.
\]
Thus the complete expected measurement vector is
\begin{equation}
  \bm\mu
  =\rho\left(\sum_{s=1}^{S}B_s\bm f_s
              +\bm b^{\mathrm{fix}}\right).
  \label{eq:additive-shard-forward}
\end{equation}
The partition is a decomposition of demand columns, not a cut of the transit
network.  Every $B_s$ is defined using complete feasible journeys and may
therefore contain paths through any stop, line, or geographical region.  The
identity is exact provided that the shards are disjoint, cover every free cell
exactly once, and use the same routing and measurement assumptions.  Frozen
demand appears once in $\bm b^{\mathrm{fix}}$ and must not be duplicated among
shards.

The adjoint decomposes compatibly.  For any measurement-space vector $\bm v$,
\[
  B^{\mathsf T}\bm v
  =\begin{bmatrix}
      B_1^{\mathsf T}\bm v\\[-1mm]
      \vdots\\[-1mm]
      B_S^{\mathsf T}\bm v
    \end{bmatrix},
\]
up to the declared ordering of the free coordinates.  Forward contributions
may therefore be accumulated independently, and the derivative with respect
to each shard uses its corresponding transpose product.

This additive structure does \emph{not} generally make the statistical
objective a sum of independent shard objectives.  For Poisson or negative-
binomial observations, the likelihood is evaluated after the shard predictions
have been summed in Equation~\eqref{eq:additive-shard-forward}.  Shards are
mathematically independent only at the linear-response stage; their parameters
remain coupled through shared measurement means and through any nonseparable
prior or demand model.

\subsection{Exact Reduced and Fixed-Routing Operators}

For free demand $\bm f_{\mathcal U}$ and positive frozen demand
$\bm f_{\mathcal F}$, the fixed-routing prediction is
\[
 \bm\mu=\rho\left(A_{\mathcal U}\bm f_{\mathcal U}
                 +A_{\mathcal F}\bm f_{\mathcal F}\right)
        =\rho\left(A_{\mathcal U}\bm f_{\mathcal U}
                 +\bm b^{\mathrm{fix}}\right).
\]
Frozen-zero cells have neither columns nor offset contributions. The matrix
$A_{\mathcal U}$ need not be materialized: an operator is mathematically
defined by the products $\bm f\mapsto A_{\mathcal U}\bm f$ and
$\bm v\mapsto A_{\mathcal U}^{\mathsf T}\bm v$ satisfying
\[
 \bm v^{\mathsf T}(A_{\mathcal U}\bm f)
 =\bm f^{\mathsf T}(A_{\mathcal U}^{\mathsf T}\bm v).
\]

If response columns fall into exact equivalence classes $e(c)$, with one
representative column in $\bar B$, then
\[
 B\bm f=\bar B\bar{\bm f},
 \qquad
 \bar f_e=\sum_{c:e(c)=e}f_c.
\]
This compression changes neither the external demand coordinates nor their
gradients. For a linear reduced parameterization $\bm f=\Phi\bm z$, the direct
parameter response is $H=B\Phi$, and its adjoint is
$H^{\mathsf T}=\Phi^{\mathsf T}B^{\mathsf T}$.
